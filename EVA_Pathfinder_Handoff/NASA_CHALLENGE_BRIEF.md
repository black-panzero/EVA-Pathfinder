## Challenge

NASA has explored Mars robotically for decades, studying its extreme environment, mapping its surface, and collecting many types of data. Far from home, the first astronauts to set foot on Mars will want the best map possible, with information on the details of their routes and destinations, updates on current conditions, and the data needed to complete their mission quickly and safely. The challenge is to create a **layered, integrated view of a location or route on the Martian surface** that pulls together data from **multiple NASA science missions** and could help a human explorer plan and carry out a successful Marswalk while conducting science.

## Background (faithful summary of user-provided text)

Human explorers can conduct observations and science activities robots may not perform, using judgment and contextual reasoning in real time. Robotic Mars missions have created a rich record of observations but not always the hyperlocal integrated combinations needed for human spacewalk mission planning. Mars Trek and USGS-NASA planetary geologic mapping are important existing tools but often emphasize regional/global views rather than astronaut-scale excursions.

## Objectives (user-provided)

- Create a map, tool or interface a Mars astronaut could use during a surface exploration mission, based on NASA data and tools.
- Choose scientific goals and a location with data relevant to the selected goals, perhaps a well-studied rover region.
- Consider **at least three kinds** of data, such as optical, thermal, spectral, topographic, geophysical, atmospheric, radiation or chemical, integrated in ways that aid navigation. This is suggested, not mandatory.
- Consider astronaut safety: radiation/weather/space weather information, mobility in spacesuits, equipment, a route and avoidance of significant obstacles such as large rocks.
- Present science destinations with useful contextual observations, not arbitrary waypoints.

## Potential considerations

- Could be habitat mission planning interface, astronaut tablet, spacesuit HUD, static snapshot or interactive digital tool.
- Can show specific historical, predicted or timeless terrain/geology information; label time scope.
- Ground images from NASA surface missions can give a point-of-view sense of destinations.
- Consider rover/robot assistance and astronaut teams.
- NASA has to be credited; third-party resource use must comply with relevant data parameters.
